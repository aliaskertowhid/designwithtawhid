Project Overview

One of America's most held cards still runs on a login page.

The Walmart Credit Card, issued in two forms, a Walmart-only store card and a Mastercard usable anywhere, is one of the most widely held retail credit cards in the US, carried by millions of Walmart shoppers.

Despite that scale, there is no dedicated app for the card at all, not even one folded into a bank's product suite. Cardholders manage their account entirely through the issuing bank's website, in a browser, on both desktop and mobile.

That means no home screen, no push notifications, no biometric login, and no on-the-go card lock. The preview of account information is limited to a balance snapshot, with no real transaction history and no proper payments interface, compared to what other major card issuers already offer natively.

This case study explores a purpose-built, native Walmart Credit Card app: one home for balance, payments, and rewards, designed around how people actually shop at Walmart — in stores, on Walmart.com, and through Walmart Pay.

Why Build This

What a browser tab cannot do that a native app can.

Right now, checking a balance means opening a browser, finding the login page, and signing in every time. We set out to change that.

  • Replace browser-only access with a real app: biometric login, push notifications, and a persistent home screen.
  • Put balance, due date, and full transaction history in front, not a limited preview.
  • Give payments a proper interface: scheduling, autopay, and partial payments, not a bare web form.
  • Merge the two card types into one clear account view.
  • Connect card activity to real Walmart context: purchases, Walmart Plus, and in-store receipts.
  • Make rewards visible and redeemable in as few taps as possible.
Design Process, MoSCoW Prioritization

Deciding what the first release actually needs.

With a long list of possible features, we used the MoSCoW method to separate what the app must have from what it could wait for.

Must Have

Non-negotiable

  • Native app replacing browser access
  • Balance and due date on the home screen
  • Full, searchable transaction history
  • One-tap card payments
  • Biometric login
Should Have

High value, not launch-blocking

  • Real-time rewards tracking
  • Push alerts for due dates and declines
  • Unified view of both card types
  • Autopay and payment scheduling
Could Have

Nice additions, later phases

  • Walmart Pay integration
  • Walmart Plus perks surfaced in app
  • Spend insights by category
  • In-app card lock and unlock
Won't Have — This Phase

Out of scope for now

  • Loan or financing products
  • Support for other retailers' cards
  • Live agent chat inside the app
  • Multi-currency support
Desk Research

What people already say about managing this card.

There is no app to review, so we looked at credit card forums, review sites, and complaint boards. A handful of composite themes, paraphrased and anonymized, kept repeating.

ACCOUNT FRICTIONCOMPOSITE THEMES, WEB AND FORUMS

Declines and disputes explained with little clarity

These are not quotes from any single named reviewer. They are recurring themes distilled from public discussion of the existing Walmart Credit Card experience, used here to ground the problem in something real rather than assumed.

Two things stood out above the rest: the total absence of a native app, and how little of the account is actually visible once a user does log in. Both became the core problems this concept had to solve.

Findings

Problems we found, and the solutions we proposed.

Problems we found

  • No dedicated app. Every check-in starts with a browser login, with no biometrics and no push notifications.
  • Web account preview is limited to a balance snapshot with little to no real transaction history.
  • Payments run through a bare web form with no scheduling, autopay, or partial payments.
  • Rewards tracking sits in a separate flow from balance and payments.
  • Account restrictions, declines, and disputes are opaque, with little explanation.
  • No link between Walmart Pay, Walmart Plus membership, and card benefits.
  • Two card types create confusion about where each can be used.

Solutions we proposed

  • A native app with biometric login and push notifications, scoped entirely to this card.
  • A full, searchable transaction history and a proper payments interface.
  • Scheduling, autopay, and partial payments to match competing card apps.
  • Rewards merged into the same view as spending, with real-time balance and one-tap redemption.
  • Plain-language status on every dispute and decline, with proactive alerts.
  • Walmart Pay and Walmart Plus perks surfaced directly where they intersect with the card.
  • Both card types presented under one account with a clear indicator of where each works.

Competitive Analysis

Benchmarked against retail cards and full banking apps.

Nine apps, each reviewed for what works, what does not, and what it means for a Walmart-specific card experience.

A
Amazon Prime Store Card
Pros

Lives directly inside the Amazon app. Rewards redeem instantly at checkout with no separate login.

Cons

Easy to overlook inside a massive shopping app. No dedicated budgeting or spend-insights view.

Opportunity

Give the card equal billing as a standalone app, not a buried feature.

T
Target RedCard
Pros

Automatic 5% savings at checkout with zero manual redemption. Clean brand integration.

Cons

Fewer account tools than full banking apps. Support routed through a separate partner.

Opportunity

Borrow the zero-effort discount model, and add the statement and payment tooling RedCard lacks.

AC
Apple Card
Pros

The UX benchmark: color-coded spend categories, real-time cash tracking, jargon-free interest breakdowns.

Cons

Tied to Apple's ecosystem. Only one card type, so no store-specific rewards logic to reference.

Opportunity

Adopt plain-language billing, adapted for two card types across iOS and Android.

R
Rogers Bank
Pros

Balance, limit, and available credit shown on one card-shaped view. Statements are a first-class tab.

Cons

Dated visual design with dense text lists. Rewards feel like an afterthought in a separate tab.

Opportunity

Keep the single card-view idea, with modern visual hierarchy instead of dense lists.

N
Neo Financial
Pros

Insight-driven experience with color-coded spend categories, real-time cashback, and personalized prompts.

Cons

Insights can bury the basics. The dark theme with many small elements can feel busy at a glance.

Opportunity

Borrow the personalized tone, but keep balance and due date as the clear headline.

C
CIBC Mobile Banking
Pros

A warm "Good morning" home screen with balance and cashback shown together, plus one-tap quick actions.

Cons

Promotional offers compete with core account info. Most navigation is for unrelated bank products.

Opportunity

Adopt the personalized greeting and quick actions, and skip the unrelated cross-sell clutter.

CO
Capital One Mobile
Pros

A comprehensive manage-card menu covering payments, statements, autopay, controls, alerts, and rewards.

Cons

A strong real-world benchmark, but generic and multi-product, not built around Walmart context.

Opportunity

Match its depth of controls, but connect it to real Walmart shopping data.

PC
PC Financial
Pros

A strong, consistent brand identity, with rewards shown as a large, always-visible number.

Cons

Card art and quick actions push transaction detail below the fold. Multi-account switching adds a layer of navigation.

Opportunity

Lean into Walmart's equally strong blue and yellow identity in the same confident way.

S
Scotiabank
Pros

A confident onboarding sequence and a bold, high-contrast balance display once inside.

Cons

Heavy marketing messaging adds friction for a returning user who just wants their balance.

Opportunity

Use confident onboarding once, then a fast, marketing-free path for returning users.

Key Lessons

What this project reinforced.

01

A card people use constantly deserves an experience scoped to that one relationship, not a browser tab.

02

Reward visibility has a direct effect on how worthwhile a card feels. Hiding it undermines the whole value proposition.

03

Transparency during friction moments — declines, disputes, and restrictions — matters more to trust than almost any single feature.

04

Connecting a financial product to the real context it is used in makes it feel considered, not generic.

Learnings

Personal takeaways from the process.

→

Designing for an existing, well-known brand raises the bar for on-brand clarity. Walmart's identity had to feel present without overwhelming financial UI conventions.

→

Benchmarking against nine real apps, not just the obvious ones, set a much clearer standard for what modern actually means in this category.

→

Grounding a speculative concept in real research made the problem framing far more credible than starting from assumptions.

→

MoSCoW prioritization made scope conversations concrete, turning a long feature wish list into a clear first release.
